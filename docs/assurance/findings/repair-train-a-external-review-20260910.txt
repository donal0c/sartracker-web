Verdict: request changes

The design intent is right and the hard part — one session retaining evidence custody across transport replacement — is genuinely well thought through. But there are five confirmed defects that bite in normal operation, three of them in the failure paths this PR exists to make safe. Notably, the PR's own claim of "no remaining accepted P1/P2" after four independent reviews doesn't hold up.

Blockers

1. Mission evidence is dropped at shutdown — start-tracking-runtime.ts:1141
if (retirementFailure !== null) throw retirementFailure sits before unregisterMissionEvidenceSettler() (:1142), deferredMissionEvidence.settleForStop() (:1143) and trackingCacheWriteLane.settle() (:1146). One failed retiring stop() during a settings save — and the latch at :1014 never clears — means accepted-but-unpersisted fixes are silently lost on app exit. That's the exact custody guarantee the PR is built to add. stopPromise ??= (:1088) memoises the rejection so it can't be retried.

2. The runtime becomes undisposable — start-app-runtime.ts:281
That rejection propagates, skipping lines 282–286: coreFeatureRuntimes, the coverage and breadcrumb-dot runtimes all leak, and disposalPromise ??= (:274) caches the rejection permanently. Same pattern in the startup catch at :257, where it additionally replaces the original startup error before any cleanup runs.

3. Mission pause falsely reports OFFLINE and enters backoff — polling-manager.ts:998
I traced this end to end. reserveCapacity throws on abort (deferred-mission-evidence.ts:110) where waitForCapacity resolved. The try at :996 has only a finally, so the throw bypasses the stopping || discardSupersededPoll guard at :1005 and lands in the catch at :1209. On mission pause, requestPollNow (:1626) aborts with stopping === false and an unchanged historyResetKey, so discardSupersededPoll returns false — the selected poller then publishes OFFLINE MODE — showing last known positions, increments consecutiveFailures, and schedules exponential backoff. A false offline banner is precisely the wrong signal here.

4. Coordinate rejections vanish on every poller stop — start-app-runtime.ts:427
suppressOperationalPublication is driven by the polling manager's own stopping flag (polling-manager.ts:750), not by retirement. And rejectionEvidenceDelivery is null in production — I grepped the repo: recordIngestRejections exists only as an optional type (tauri-mission-store.ts:1214) and in test mocks, no store implements it. So the suppressed branch is a no-op and the applyCurrentPositionRejections fallback never runs. Master surfaced those rejections; this drops them.

5. Stationary attention under-reports and flickers on ordinary GPS noise
The anchor resets on any single fix ≥ threshold from anchor or previous, with no corroboration requirement — unlike the ≥500 m gross-excursion path, which does require it. Measured against a reimplementation of master:

What holds up

prepareStationaryAttention is exactly equivalent to evaluateStationaryAttention — I fuzzed 400 sequences × 14 appends with random lat/lon/accuracy and duplicate timestamps, zero mismatches. The incremental projection is sound; it just faithfully preserves the algorithm's own flaw. The retired-poller-publishes race, the retiringPollers set/delete ordering, the reloadTail/reloadGeneration interlock, and exactly-once capacity release were each traced and cleared. 5cd9acbe's fail-closed proof change is a real improvement. Scoped run: 9 tracking test files, 241 tests, green.

The escape analysis worth recording: no test asserts elapsedMs against ground truth under jitter, and disposal-while-reconfiguring, a throwing reconfigure, and retirementFailure surfacing have zero coverage — which is why four review passes missed all of this.
